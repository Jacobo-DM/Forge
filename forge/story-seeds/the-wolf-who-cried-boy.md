# The Wolf Who Cried, Boy  
## A protected story seed / DiscoveryMode threshold prompt

Also held as: **When the Wolf Cries, Boy**

This seed is not yet a finished story.

It is a threshold device about being misread, being named dangerous, and carrying a cry that may not yet have become speech.

The title holds two doors:

**When the Wolf Cries, Boy**  
The cry has not fully happened yet. It is future, conditional, suspended — a warning, a wobble, a question.

**The Wolf Who Cried, Boy**  
The cry has already happened. Something was said, or almost said, and “Boy” remains unresolved — name, warning, plea, accusation, memory.

The question should remain unstable for now:

Who is the wolf?

For some readers, the wolf may be the boy.  
For others, the father.  
For others, the feared one.  
For others, the part of themselves that learned to hide before it had language.

## Core Prompt

We all feel a lump in the throat from time to time.

How does it find release?

What does it mean to carry a wound and hold it in silence?

What changes when the wound is given a voice, a song, a pen, or a place to dance?

## Guardrail

This prompt does not ask anyone to tell their wound before they are ready.

It asks where feeling goes when it has not yet become speech.

## Possible responses

A reader may answer with:

- a sound
- a drawing
- a gesture
- a sentence
- a silence
- a walk
- a rhythm
- a story beginning
- nothing yet

**Nothing yet** is not non-participation.  
It is a valid form of presence.

## Notes for future development

Do not over-explain the wolf.

Do not turn the title into a clever reversal of the old fable.

Do not make the prompt perform tenderness while extracting.

Avoid facilitation language that pressures sharing by saying “it is safe to share.” Safety cannot be declared into existence by the facilitator.

The stronger inquiry is not:

“What is your wound?”

It is:

“Where does feeling go when it has not yet become speech?”
