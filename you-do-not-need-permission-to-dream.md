# You Do Not Need Permission to Dream

> You do not need permission to dream.

This root note names a principle shared across Fox’s Anvil, DiscoveryMode, MythGear, MythWear, Regenerative Gaming, and the wider Flower Road commons.

The point is not fantasy without responsibility. The point is that the right to imagine has not been outsourced — not to institutions, markets, algorithms, credentials, or approved subject categories.

A child should be allowed to imagine a raft that is also a classroom, a garment that is also a tool, a game that is also a repair practice, a story that is also a way of learning, or an AI that is not a master or replacement but a partner in noticing.

This belongs in the Forge because the Forge holds the deeper ethic behind the tools. Fox’s Anvil gives the principle practical bodies: the raft, the signal finder, the sack tool, the field logs. DiscoveryMode gives it to children as a way of seeing. MythGear and MythWear carry it into body-scale tools and repairable forms.

> The right to imagine has not been outsourced.

This does not mean every dream should be built immediately. It means the right to imagine has not been outsourced.

The work begins there: with the restoration of permission, followed by the practice of care.
